# SOLR-07 V5 queue quality and offline V6 continuation plan

**Status, 2026-10-07:** analysis only. The original `AddReplicaTest.testAddMultipleReplicas` JUnit and V5 controller continue unchanged. V6 must not activate unless V5 ends naturally with fewer than 80 effective trials. This document is a point-in-time forecast and an executable handoff specification, not a replacement live checkpoint.

The 60 passing runs averaged 17.05 seconds; three 300-second timeouts contributed 900.28 seconds.

The final 17 selections through 347 produced one effective result; most were depth-3 descendants of parents 146/147 or depth-4 descendants of parent 172. Parents 146/147 themselves passed and retained the target, but their next siblings repeatedly applied only one of three planned faults. This is a local low-yield streak, not evidence that the whole remaining queue has the same rate. `campaign_v5.choose` first forces any entry aged at least 20 rounds and breaks equal-age ties by plan identity. All frozen backfill entries had aged to 77 rounds, so this gate can exhaust siblings from one parent before the family/parent balance and runtime feedback matter.

At the same SHA, 210 queued plans remained: depths 2/3/4/5 were 93/81/25/11; 198 had a passing/effective parent verdict and 12 were fallback descendants. A read-only simulation of `choose` over that fixed queue, without inventing outcomes or adding new descendants, selected 165 plans with depths 90/53/15/7. All 165 came from already frozen V4 parent traces. This is an **ordering forecast**, not a simulation of future faults or coverage.

A later independent read at state SHA-256 `ef1ac0991bc07b1cf9c3882182ebba82b5a4e1af810c5ae59bad1ff2bfbf8b50` found selected 359, **67 effective total**, 207 queued. V5 had then contributed 16 effective out of 89 selections. Thirteen more effective results were needed in the remaining 153 selected slots: an 8.50% minimum future rate. For the earlier 347 snapshot the requirement was 16/165 = 9.70%. If every future attempt had a fixed independent success probability equal to the 359-snapshot V5 rate (16/89 = 18.0%), the binomial probability of reaching 80 by selected 512 is about **99.97%**. A beta-binomial posterior predictive calculation with a uniform prior, Beta(17,74), gives **98.99%**. Under a 5% future rate the same probability is only **4.4%**. These are conditional sensitivity calculations: attempts share parents, queue priorities change, and newly generated descendants change the mix. They do not justify an unconditional claim that 80 will be met. At the measured mean, another 153 attempts cost roughly 81 minutes of run time; three similar timeout clusters can increase that appreciably.

## New root inventory, with the time window kept distinct

Using only passing, exact, effective V5 runs, each candidate was required to be an `IO_OBSERVED` entry on the selected target actor and the **same SolrZkClient identity** as a later exact `TARGET_CHECK` with `corePresent=true`, before that guard. At the 359 checkpoint SHA above, this produced 90 distinct root plan identities absent from completed and queued V5 plans. Eighty-seven appeared in at least two passing runs. Of the 90, **19** appeared after the final applied fault and before the guard; **15** of those appeared in that window in at least two passing runs. Seventy-one appeared only before the final fault, and two appeared in both windows across runs. The 19 are the observed recovery-window tier. The 71 are new plan identities, but their observed position does not show new recovery behavior. The two healthy calibration traces shared 90 root identities, and all 90 have already been queued or tested. Eighty-two of the 90 new V5 roots appeared in healthy-02 alone and none in healthy-01; they cannot be called stable in both healthy seeds.

This inventory is provisional until V5 naturally ends and all source traces are SHA-pinned again. A faulted run can expose a root point that is absent from a fresh unfaulted replay. Exact runtime matching in V6, not inventory membership, decides whether that plan was exercised.

## V6 activation gate: only after a natural V5 stop

1. Accept only a stopped V5 checkpoint with `stop_reason` equal to `selected_trial_cap`, `queue_exhausted`, or `plan_exhausted`, **and total effective < 80**. Confirm the V5 controller/JUnit JVM exited and `trial-(selected_count+1)` does not exist. If V5 reaches 80, leave V6 inactive.
2. Freeze the exact state bytes as `handoff-v6/state-N-SHA.json`, record their SHA-256, and generate a read-only raw manifest for every completed trial: `events.jsonl`, `fault.properties`, `jacoco.exec`, `junit.log`, `run.json`, `trial.json`. Run an independent audit requiring `selected_count == len(trials) == len(seen)`, unique completed/queue identities, matching strict fault plan and raw applied count, actor/client binding validity, and `errors=[]`. Pin the V5 scheduler, original runner, agent, original Solr revision, and two healthy calibration hashes. Re-read the state bytes before committing the handoff to reject a concurrent change.
3. Build an immutable inventory from the frozen traces. For each candidate record the complete `(target identity, ordered fault sequence)` selector, source trial numbers, event SHA-256, observed event and target-guard sequence numbers, physical node/client IDs **as evidence only**, test outcome, phase (`post_last_fault_pre_guard` or `pre_fault_pre_guard`), family, and source run elapsed time. Deduplicate against all completed and queued identities. Compare with the V5 frozen backfill inventory and label those older plans `legacy_backfill`, never V6 discovery; they may remain eligible in the exploration lane. SHA-pin the inventory and perform a second independent audit. If no admissible untested plan exists, record `plan_exhausted`; do not activate V6.
4. Commit a V6 metadata block only after those audits pass and an exclusive controller lock is held. It records `policy=v6_shallow_observed_io`, the immutable V5 checkpoint and inventory SHA values, scheduler code hash, `activated_utc`, and the explicit new budget. Never reuse or overwrite a completed trial number. If a controller dies after writing a trial directory but before state commit, reconcile that raw directory manually and retain it as an interrupted attempt; do not rerun its ID.

## V6 candidate and scheduling rule

Keep the original bytecode probe, actor binding, strict I/O selector, target oracle, and JaCoCo feedback. Do not build or require a per-trial causal graph. The new generation rule applies only to **depth-one roots and depth-two pairs**:

- **Tier 0 root:** an untested same-actor/same-client I/O point observed after the final fault and before a `corePresent=true` exact guard in at least two independent passing/effective runs. The current provisional tier has 15 identities.
- **Tier 1 root:** the same post-fault window witnessed once (currently four), then pre-fault points witnessed in at least two passing runs (the 71-point provisional tier). Preserve this phase label; do not credit pre-fault points as recovery novelty.
- **Pair:** append one observed I/O only to a **completed, strict, effective, JUnit-passing one-fault parent** whose raw trace shows that I/O after its injected fault and before a same-client `corePresent=true` guard. A depth-two or deeper trace is not a witness for deleting a prefix fault. New V6 roots can yield such parents after their first exact trial. Existing V4 one-fault parent plans absent from V5 completed and queue remain `legacy_backfill`, not a new-discovery tier.

Use four of every five selections for the shallow root/pair pool and one for other still-valid V5 queued or legacy-backfill plans or lower-tier exploration; if a lane is empty, fill from the other. In the shallow lane sort by tier, fewest completed trials in the I/O family, fewest children tested from the immediate parent, greater JaCoCo/I/O novelty of a passing parent, and lower median elapsed time for that family/depth; use bounded wait age and stable plan identity as tie breakers. Never let wait age alone override the tier. Allow at most two consecutive children of the same parent and four of the same I/O family while alternatives exist. Penalize recurring 300-second timeout families in the cost component, while retaining the exploration lane. These rules address V5's contiguous low-yield parent streak without discarding potential multi-fault behavior.

For every V6 trial record wall-clock start/end and elapsed seconds, the exact per-step actor/client/site/operation/resource/hit match, target guard and throw after the final fault, original JUnit exit/failure text, new JaCoCo probes, and new I/O families. The current event stream has sequence numbers but no event timestamps; sequence distance to the guard may rank proximity, but it must not be reported as elapsed time or proof that the guard ran before JUnit teardown. A target throw remains a **candidate** pending raw log/source review and strict replay, as in the V4 five-throw and V5 trial-298 reviews.

## Versioned budget and decision thresholds

At the V5 stop, let `N=selected_count` and `E=effective_count` from the audited frozen checkpoint. Set V6 `effective_trials=80`, `max_selected=N+256` (therefore at most 768 in total), and retain `max_faults=8` for any existing V5 sequence; V6's *new* root/pair generation never exceeds depth two. Persist these exact values and the deficit `80-E` in the V6 metadata. Stop at 80 effective, 256 new selected trials, or genuine queue/inventory exhaustion, and write a terminal raw audit with per-trial elapsed time. Do not silently extend this budget or call a timeout/teardown target throw a new bug.

While V5 runs, a dashboard can **prepare** V6 if a completed 40-trial window has fewer than four effective results, or a beta-binomial forecast from the last 60 completed trials gives less than 20% chance of filling `80-E` within `512-N`. That flag must not stop or modify V5. The only execution gate for V6 remains the natural V5 terminal checkpoint with `E<80`, successful immutable audit, and a nonempty distinct inventory. If V6's first 32 selections yield fewer than two effective trials and no new coverage/I/O family, re-rank its remaining untested families in a new checkpointed scheduler version; do not increase depth or replay old IDs implicitly.
