# SOLR-09 full-role node1 timeout candidate triage

Verdict: **exact target exception reproduced, but no new correctness bug established**. The two injected exceptions force the two Boolean waits to return `false` at their entry points. The resulting `DeleteReplicaCmd.deleteCore` exception is the direct, intended result of that control flow. This campaign should be recorded as a target-exception observation with `new_bug=false`, not as a confirmed Solr defect.

The unchanged Solr 8.11.4 `DeleteReplicaTest.deleteReplicaAndVerifyDirectoryCleanup` creates a four-node cluster and a two-replica collection, then deletes its leader and expects the replacement leader and directories to reflect the deletion (`DeleteReplicaTest.java:149–170`). The healthy r0000 and one-fault r0001 JUnit runs each passed one test with zero failures. The full node1 campaign ended `queue_exhausted` after five trials in 78.426 seconds; r0002 failed and r0003/r0004 reproduced the same failure.

At original `OverseerCollectionMessageHandler.java:432–447`, `waitForCoreNodeGone` calls `zkStateReader.waitForState` for up to 30 seconds, catches `TimeoutException`, and returns `false`. At original `DeleteReplicaCmd.java:254–283`, the command performs an `UNLOAD`, calls that helper, queues `DELETECORE` as fallback, calls the helper again, and throws `Could not remove replica` if both results are `false`. The outer catch wraps this in `Error waiting for corenode gone`. The injected exception is at `waitForCoreNodeGone @434`, directly before `waitForState`; it does not represent a failed ZooKeeper I/O operation or an elapsed 30-second wait.

In r0002, the typed `TimeoutException` faults applied at events 280 and 285; the exact target check took its failure branch at 286 and the `SolrException` was thrown at 287. All four events have the same node2, epoch 0, `deleteCore` occurrence, task context, and Overseer thread. r0003 and r0004 have the same event order and same-occurrence identity on their respective nodes. All three selected JUnit runs failed with `Error waiting for corenode gone`; the r0001 one-fault control passed. `UNLOAD` itself returned HTTP status 0 in all three failing runs. The delete request completed in only 66–75 ms, whereas each unmodified wait is configured for 30 seconds, confirming that the test skipped both real waits by throwing at entry.

The campaign's `qualified_target_throw_evidence` and replay records correctly identify the target exception; they do not establish that a replica remained, a fresh ZooKeeper state contradicted the error, or a real I/O failure caused stale coordination state. The prior CrashFuzz SOLR-09 `CANDIDATE-059.md` describes a different, lower-level three-fault stale-reader hypothesis with unresolved upstream novelty; these immediate helper-timeout trials neither validate nor refute that hypothesis. For a correctness claim, inject at a lower-level ZooKeeper I/O door and verify the final replica and fresh state after the real wait or its recovery path.
