# HBASE-32 V3 final novelty triage (2026-10-07)

This is a read-only classification review of the three frozen role-aware diagnostic runs. The detailed raw/actor/lock and hash comparison is in [the independent three-run audit](hbase32-role-v3-three-run-independent-review-2026-10-07.md). No JUnit was run and no case status or fuzz denominator was changed.

**Observed:** f001 and f002, on different meta-host RegionServers and region replicas, each applied the same two-fault semantic plan with exact same-raw-region B7 and left the unchanged `TestRegionReplicaFailover#testLotsOfRegionReplicas` JUnit unfinished at the 600 s cap (641.95/643.45 s including shutdown). Their 130–500 s dumps and trace prove the same node's `ServerStateNode` READ held by a transition RPC while `RegionServerTracker` waits for WRITE, the RPC waits for a region lock, and its PEWorker owner retries a synchronous `hbase:meta` write to the failed meta host. The first `carryingMeta=true` SCP was submitted only after timeout shutdown released the READ. f003 applied the same frozen plan/agent/JAR/JUnit with strict actor/target identity and direct B7, but READ holders were zero at WRITE; SCP started immediately, meta reopened, all 600 child assignments completed, and JUnit passed in 89.83 s. This is a scheduling-dependent **pre-SCP recovery wait**, not a failure caused by the target B7 exception alone.

**Permanence is unproven.** In f002 the particular `PEWorker-5` meta `HTable.put` had reached try **36 of 46** at `role-f002.log:31715` immediately before the harness's 600 s shutdown (`:32415`). HBase 2.6.6 `RegionStateStore.updateRegionLocation:231-249` catches a final `IOException`, aborts the master, and rethrows; `RegionRemoteProcedureBase.execute:337-346` backs off only after the synchronous put returns or throws. The test was killed before retry exhaustion, so these observations establish ten-minute noncompletion and the dependency chain, **not** an infinite JVM deadlock, permanent cluster nonprogress, or what would happen after the final retry. Even later master abort would be a separate outcome requiring direct observation. The f003 pass is a valid negative control for schedule sensitivity, not proof that the f001/f002 state would eventually recover.

**Known-issue boundary:** ASF [HBASE-24526](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/HBASE-24526?focusedCommentId=17149647) remains **Open / Unresolved**, with no fix version in the official Jira API. It documents a meta assignment procedure that has **already started**, but an `openRegion` subprocedure receives no RPC because procedure workers are blocked on non-meta `hbase:meta` writes; the report describes over 15 minutes without self-recovery. Its linked [HBASE-24673](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/HBASE-24673) is also **Open / Unresolved** and proposes yielding non-meta transition procedures while meta is unavailable. The V3 data has a more specific, different ordering: `AssignmentManager.submitServerCrash` cannot acquire its `ServerStateNode` WRITE, so the dead meta host's SCP has **not yet been submitted**. The known issue therefore overlaps the meta-write/procedure starvation mechanism and symptom, but its public description does not establish this exact pre-SCP lock cycle as either a duplicate or a separate root cause. [HBASE-21508](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/HBASE-21508) introduced the READ/WRITE transition-report fence to prevent dead-server reports from racing with SCP; that fence remains in 2.6.6 and the local `origin/branch-2.6` snapshot `03be5bee0ade1072258189bc9e28c12f193bab4b`. [HBASE-29259 comment 17943805](https://issues.apache.org/jira/browse/HBASE-29259?focusedCommentId=17943805) mentions non-meta meta writes waiting for SCP, but its resolved main failure is an orphaned procedure during master restart after SCP/TRSP already run concurrently.

**Verdict:** retain `scheduling_sensitive_recovery_wait_candidate`, `new_bug=false`, novelty unconfirmed. A defensible new-bug classification would require observation beyond the relevant retry/operation bound without harness shutdown, or a same-version causal source differential that frees the pre-SCP wait under the same exact actor plan, followed by an issue-level comparison showing HBASE-24526/HBASE-24673 do not cover this mechanism. No source or bounded trace currently proves permanence or nonduplication.
