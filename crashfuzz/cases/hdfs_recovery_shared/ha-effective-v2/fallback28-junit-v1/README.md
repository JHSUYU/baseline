# HDFS-28 alternate original HA JUnit campaign

This directory runs an independent campaign on released Hadoop 3.4.3 `TestPipelinesFailover#testLeaseRecoveryAfterFailover`. It is a source-reviewed fallback for the HDFS-28 `BlockRecoveryWorker.getDatanodeID` guard. Its two healthy runs and fault budget have no overlap with the current `testFailoverRightBeforeCommitSynchronization` HA ledger, the older `TestBlockRecovery2` phase, or their denominators.

`healthy_gate.py` invokes the existing case-local ASM/JaCoCo `run_original.py` with `--test-method testLeaseRecoveryAfterFailover`. Each seed gets its own output, MiniDFSCluster scratch, dynamic cluster ports, raw trace, JaCoCo data, JUnit log, and manifest. The calibration takes the shared `unit-test-pivot/jvm-start.lock`, waits for an active HA JUnit to finish, checks at most four existing Java processes and at least 10 GiB free, then holds the lock through this short healthy JUnit so the HA controller can resume at the next trial boundary. It never edits the current HA checkpoint, queue, seen IDs, or running JVM.

The runner is resumable at completed-seed boundaries: a completed healthy seed with a passing manifest and raw files is reused; a partial seed is left untouched for manual review. `healthy-receipt.json` records raw/source hashes. `validate_healthy.py` independently requires two passing original JUnit executions, contiguous traces, an observed 3-DataNode cluster, the same HDFS-28 source guard, an owner-bound recovery root, and RPC points before a later guard on that root in each seed. It also requires shared RPC site families and matching released test/overlay provenance. Failure of any check means this JUnit is not admitted as a fallback workload.

The source rationale and the current queue limitation are recorded in `../audits/hdfs28-original-junit-alternatives.md`. Healthy calibrations do not count as fault trials or bug observations.

## Separate resumable campaign

`campaign_v1.py` delegates the original JUnit execution and atomic checkpoint mechanics to the SHA-locked `../campaign.py`, but owns this directory's `checkpoint.json`, `runs/`, `summary.json`, `trial-ledger.csv`, and `raw-audit.json`. `run_fallback.py` selects the alternate original JUnit method. Each completed fault run keeps its own exact plan, raw trace, JUnit log, JaCoCo data, production coverage, time, and source manifest SHA. An interrupted active run resumes from the checkpoint without repeating completed trials. The shared launch gate is held only until the new JVM appears, with at most four existing Java processes and at least 10 GiB free.

The independent campaign starts from the two healthy seeds' common, actor-bound `initReplicaRecovery` HDFS_RPC family. Its initial queue contains eight fresh one-fault HDFS_RPC plans, the missed Hadoop IPC pilot plan with its retry count preserved, and eight deeper mutations from pilot 01's observed postfault I/O. The exact pilot 01 selector is in `seen` and cannot run again. The two pilots do not contribute to the new effective count; pilot 01's coverage and I/O feedback initialize the new queue/novelty union. `audit_campaign.py` recomputes each exact physical owner/recovery-root match, postfault HDFS-28 guard, target throw, production and target-related coverage novelty, actor-bound I/O novelty, source/JAR provenance, and raw file SHA from the saved evidence. A missed selector is ineffective. A target throw stops the campaign for replay and source review, with no automatic new-bug claim.

The base goal is **80 effective HDFS-28 trials**, at most eight faults per sequence, at most 320 new attempts. At trial 80, only target-related BlockRecoveryWorker method/branch coverage or stable actor-bound I/O novelty in the last eight effective trials extends the goal to 128, depth 10, and 512 attempts. Exhaustion or the attempt cap is recorded as inconclusive if the goal is unmet. The initial checkpoint had 0 completed, 17 queued, and 18 unique seen IDs; `audit_campaign.py` independently passed at this zero-trial boundary. The earlier HA campaign's HDFS-28/29/30 denominators remain separate.

## Versioned recovery-RPC successor

The first 12 depth-two attempts were mostly FILE/HADOOP_IPC plans and retained the target in zero trials. `successor_v2.py` is prepared for the first controller's **natural queue-exhausted boundary**. It refuses a live controller, a reserved/running JUnit, a candidate, or a mismatched source SHA. It runs the independent full raw audit before changing state. It retains every completed trial, queue item, seen ID, effective denominator, coverage union, and fault budget, then appends only unseen one-fault `initReplicaRecovery` HDFS_RPC selectors actually observed in an effective original-JUnit trace with a physical owner and later same-root HDFS-28 check. New singleton discovery is capped at 64 across the successor. Its scheduler favors those singletons and same-actor RPC descendants, while reserving one of five selections for other queued I/O. A receipt records old/new checkpoint and source SHA, raw-audit SHA, unchanged completed/coverage hashes, and provenance for each appended selector. It can resume the new checkpoint without replaying a completed plan.

At a read-only dry-run after 40 alternate attempts, the original ledger had 14 queued/32 seen IDs and 11 effective trials. Twelve unseen actor-bound target-before HDFS_RPC singleton IDs were available; the simulated handoff kept the old queue/seen prefixes unchanged, added exactly 12 unique plans, and selected one of the new RPC singletons next. These are conditional plans: a selector from fault-exposed recovery I/O may miss in a fresh JUnit, and such a miss will remain outside the effective denominator.
